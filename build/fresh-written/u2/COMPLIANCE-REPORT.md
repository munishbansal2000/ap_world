## Repairs made (in-place, keys untouched)

The three repairs below fix a single recurring issue: fresh-written paraphrases of genuine primary sources were presented inside quotation marks as if verbatim. The substance was verified as genuine in every case; the repair changes only the attribution line to honest "Adapted from" framing. Keys, stems, options, and explanations are unchanged.

1. **Items 001–003 (Ibn Battuta / Mali stimulus):** "Ibn Battuta, a Moroccan scholar, wrote of his visit to the Mali Empire around 1352: '…'" → **"Adapted from Ibn Battuta's account of his visit to the Mali Empire around 1352: '…'"**
   - Substance verified: Quran-memorization zeal (children chained until they memorize), crowded Friday prayer, unveiled women at court, poets reciting verses at court — all confirmed in the Rihla account of Mali (human.libretexts.org course page quoting the text; Yale curriculum unit 07.02.06 noting his ambivalence — praised Quranic piety, disapproved of un-veiled women and "ridiculous" royal poets).
2. **Items 004–006 (Marco Polo / Hangzhou stimulus):** "Marco Polo, a Venetian merchant, described the city of Hangzhou …: 'The city has twelve thousand bridges…'" → **"Adapted from Marco Polo's description of Hangzhou …: 'The city has twelve thousand bridges…'"**
   - Substance verified: the 12,000-bridges figure is Polo's genuine claim about Kinsay/Hangzhou (Columbia Asia for Educators primary-source page; Britannica/LA Times), and "the inhabitants … use paper-money" is verbatim from the Polo text.
3. **Item 023 (Florentine merchant's handbook stimulus):** "From a Florentine merchant's handbook, c. 1340: '…'" → **"Adapted from Francesco Pegolotti's merchant handbook, c. 1340: '…'"**
   - Substance verified: the "road from Tana to Cathay is perfectly safe, whether by day or by night" passage is the genuine, famous opening of Pegolotti's *Pratica della Mercatura* (c. 1340); he does advise on relay stations, papers, and the money-changer details. Note: this exact PD passage is also used on real AP World exams — using a public-domain primary source is not IP risk.
